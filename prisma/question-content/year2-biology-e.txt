S|Explain the 10% rule for energy transfer.|Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level passes to the next because the rest is lost as heat in respiration, in waste and in uneaten parts.|So chains are short.|M
S|Describe the nitrogen cycle briefly.|Nitrogen is fixed by bacteria, converted to nitrates by nitrification and taken up by plants, passed along food chains, returned by decomposers as ammonia, and released to the air by denitrification.|Bacteria are essential.|M
S|Distinguish between mutualism, commensalism and parasitism.|In mutualism both benefit, in commensalism one benefits and the other is unaffected, and in parasitism one benefits and the other is harmed.|Examples: lichen, epiphyte, tapeworm.|M
S|Describe ecological succession.|It is the gradual replacement of communities, starting with pioneer species on bare ground and ending in a stable climax community.|Soil builds up.|M
S|Distinguish between a habitat and a niche.|A habitat is the place where an organism lives, while a niche is its role, including feeding, behaviour and interactions.|Address versus profession.|M
S|Describe how the size of a population can be estimated by capture-recapture.|Animals are captured, marked and released, a second sample is taken, and the population is calculated from the number marked in the first sample times the second sample size divided by the number recaptured.|Assumes no migration.|M
